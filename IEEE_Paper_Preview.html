Abstract—As cloud-native architectures grow in complexity, the demand for autonomous system management becomes critical. Traditional monitoring systems often rely on binary threshold-based alerts, which lack the context necessary to differentiate between transient glitches, resource exhaustion, and security threats. This paper introduces a V3 Self-Healing AI framework designed for high-fidelity microservice environments. By utilizing a Multi-Signal Correlation Engine within a MAPE-K loop, our system autonomously classifies and remediates system faults. Experimental results demonstrate that the proposed architecture successfully resolves 5xx error spikes and prevents "Restart Storms" during overloads.
Keywords—Self-Healing AI, Microservices, Prometheus, MAPE-K, Autonomous Remediation.

Introduction

The evolution of software engineering toward microservice-based architectures has brought unparalleled scalability but introduced significant operational complexity. Ensuring high availability in these distributed systems remains a formidable challenge. Manual intervention is often too slow and prone to human error, specially during large-scale outages.

Existing solutions like Kubernetes liveness probes provide a basic "self-healing" mechanism by restarting unhealthy containers. However, such binary health-checks are blind to "grey failures"---scenarios where a service remains "Running" but is functionally unresponsive due to deadlocks. Furthermore, blind restarts during a resource overload can exacerbate system instability.

Proposed Methodology

The heart of our system is an autonomous MAPE-K loop integrated with a high-fidelity monitoring stack. The framework consists of four primary modules: a monitored Boutique microservice, a Prometheus-based telemetry aggregator, an AI-driven controller, and a Docker-socket integrated execution agent.

The system differentiates between two critically different scenarios that both return a 503 code. Status 503 with Low CPU is classified as a deadlock, requiring an immediate restart. Status 503 with High CPU is classified as a resource overload, where the AI suppresses the restart to preserve active user sessions.

The experimental data indicates that by using a 60-second observation window, the AI achieves 100% classification accuracy across the evaluation set, significantly reducing MTTR compared to manual SRE intervention.

Conclusion

This research demonstrates that multi-signal correlation is vital for secondary-level autonomous healing. Our framework provides a blueprint for building "resilient-by-design" systems that are aware of their environmental context. Future work will explore the use of Reinforcement Learning to optimize agent behavior.

Abstract —Modern micro service applications are 
expected to run continuously, yet relying on manual 
intervention during failures often slows down recovery and 
increases operational effort. To address this issue, this 
study proposes a self -healing AI framework that 
continuously monitors API endpoints and automatically 
detects and resolves production faults. The system 
combines a rule -based layer for quickly identifying 
common errors with a deep learning model based on LSTM 
and Multi -Head Self -Attention to capture more complex 
and gradual performance irregularities. Trained on 17,375 
labelled time -series samples covering multiple fault 
scenarios, the model demonstrated strong validation 
performance using a statistically defined anomaly 
threshold. To adapt to changing runtime conditions, the 
framework incorporates online learning and dynamic 
threshold adjustment. When abnormal behaviour is 
detected, a decision engine analyzes real -time system 
metrics and initiates appropriate recovery actions, followed 
by verification checks to ensure stability. Experimental 
evaluation using containerized chaos testing confirmed fast 
detection and recovery, demonstrating the framework’s 
effectiveness for autonomous API management in modern 
cloud environments.  
Keywords — Self-Healing Systems, AI-Based Anomaly 
Detection, LSTM with Multi -Head Self -Attention, Micro 
services, Chaos Engineering, Autonomous Remediation.  
I. INTRODUCTION  
The shift toward micro service architectures has made 
production systems far more distributed and complex 
than traditional monolithic applications. With dozens or 
even hundreds of independently deployed services 
interacting in real time, manual fault detection and 
recovery are no longer practical at scale. When issues are 
not resolved quickly, the consequences extend beyond 
temporary disruption, affecting revenue, service 
reliability, and user confidence. Although operational 
practices such as Site Reliability Engineering bring 
structure to incident response, remediation still depends 
largely on human judgment, which introduces 
unavoidable delays during critical failures.  
Conventional rule -based monitoring tools can detect 
clear and well -understood problems by triggering alerts 
when predefined thresholds are crossed. While effective 
for sudden spikes or resource saturation events, these 
systems are limited in their ability to recognize gradual 
or multi -metric behavioral changes, such as slow 
memory growth or progressive latency drift. Machine learning models offer a more adaptive alternative by 
learning patterns of normal system behavior and 
identifying deviations over time. In particular, LSTM -
based sequence models enhanced with attention 
mechanisms are well suited for analyzing time -series 
telemetry and capturing subtle temporal irregularities. 
However, many existing approaches stop at anomaly 
detection and rely on human operators to decide how to 
respond, which reintroduce latency and limits scalability.  
 
To overcome these limitations, this work proposes an 
autonomous Self -Healing AI System that unifies 
detection, contextual interpretation, and remediation 
within a single closed -loop framework. The architecture 
combines rapid rule -based detection for known fault 
patterns with an LSTM model augmented by Multi -Head 
Self-Attention to capture more complex anomalies. A 
context -aware decision engine evaluates real -time 
resource indicators, such as CPU utilization, to determine 
the most appropriate corrective action and avoid 
counterproductive responses. The system also 
incorporates online learning and dynamic threshold 
adjustment to remain effective as production conditions 
evolve. Experimental validation using controlled 
infrastructure -level chaos testing demonstrates reliable 
detection, verified recovery, and low false -positive rates, 
supporting the feasibility of autonomous remediation in 
modern cloud -native environments.  
II. RELATED WORK  
F. Merah and Y. Benabdellah .,[1] work addresses the 
challenge of identifying abnormal behavior in satellite 
systems by focusing on real -time anomaly detection 
within large volumes of telemetry data. Satellite 
telemetry parameters provide continuous insight into 
system health and performance, making timely detection 
of irregular patterns essential for reliable operation. The 
approach applies machine learning –based time series 
analysis to compare predicted telemetry values with 
actual observation s in order to identify anomalous 
events. The methodology includes structured 
preprocessing steps such as handling missing data, 
normalization, and scaling, followed by the use of 
predictive models including autoregressive methods, 
deep learning –based sequence models, and probabilistic 
forecasting techniques. Experimental evaluation 
demonstrates that the forecasting -based model delivers 
consistent and accurate anomaly detection with low 
computational overhead, outperforming other considered 
approaches. Overall, the study highlights the 
effectiveness of time series –driven machine learning 
frameworks for efficient and reliable anomaly detection 
in satellite telemetry systems.  
S. Yu, H. Wu, X. Niu and C. Nie .,[2] study provides a 
detailed examination of fault injection testing as a 
resilience assessment approach for microservice -based 
systems. The objective of the work is to analyze how 
fault injection techniques are used to evaluate the 
correctness and robustness of fault -tolerance 
mechanisms, given the lack of a consolidated overview 
of existing practices. Through systematic observation of 
current methods, the study outlines a generalized fault 
injection testing framework and identifies key 
components that represent important design 
considerations. It further reviews how existing 
techniques address the challenges associated with these 
components and discusses observed limitations. The 
study concludes by highlighting potential directions for 
future research aimed at improving fault injection testing 
strategies for reliable microservice systems.  
B. Xu, T. Ding and L. Guo .,[3] study addresses the 
limitations of conventional Signal -to-Noise Ratio (SNR) 
estimation techniques by proposing a deep learning –
based architecture designed to improve accuracy and 
adaptability across various modulation schemes. The 
objective is to enhance sequential signal modeling by 
incorporating a multi -head self -attention mechanism 
within a spatial bidirectional LSTM framework, allowing 
the model to better capture temporal dependencies and 
contextual relationships. In addition, convolutional layers 
are integrated to strengthen feature extraction and 
improve representation of critical signal characteristics. 
The resulting attention -based convolutional BiLSTM 
architecture is evaluated using a simulated 
communication signal dataset containing multiple SNR 
levels and modulation types. Experimental findings 
demonstrate improved estimation performance, 
robustness, and broader modulation applicability 
compared to baseline approaches, highlighting the 
effectiveness of the proposed design for advanced SNR 
estimation tasks.  
H. Wu, Z. Yu, B. Quan, Y. Zhang, Y. He and J. Li .,[4] 
study focuses on improving the reliability and resilience 
of distribution networks by addressing the limitations of 
conventional self -healing control methods, particularly 
slow response and partial recovery. The proposed 
approach adopts a multi -agent system –based control 
strategy in which individual agents are designed with 
sensing, decision -making, and execution capabilities to 
enable coordinated fault management. By formulating 
collaborative control rules and optimized recovery 
objectives, the strategy supports adaptive network 
reconfiguration following fault occurrences. The 
approach is validated through simulation under multiple 
fault scenarios using a standard test network and an 
integrated simulation environment. Performance 
evaluation demonstrates faster fault detection, improved 
recovery efficiency, and stronger communication 
robustness when compared with traditional self -healing 
control techniques, confirming the effectiveness of the 
proposed strategy.  A. Chopra  et al.,[5] study has the objective of improving 
predictive maintenance in Industrial Internet of Things 
environments by enabling real -time detection of 
abnormal system behavior. The proposed framework 
applies a combination of machine learning and deep 
learning models to continuously analyze sensor data 
streams and identify potential faults. By leveraging both 
feature extraction and temporal learning capabilities, the 
approach supports timely and reliable anomaly 
identification. Experimental evaluation demonstrates 
high detection accuracy, strong classification 
performance, low false alarm rates, and reduced 
detection latency. The findings indicate that AI -based 
anomaly detection effectively enhances real -time 
monitoring and maintenance efficiency in industrial 
systems.  
III. PROPOSED SYSTEM  
The proposed system follows a sidecar monitoring 
architecture in which all observability, inference, and 
remediation logic operate independently of the target 
API service. The monitored FastAPI application runs 
without modification, unaware of monitoring or fault 
injection activities. This separation ensures portability, 
transparency, and non -intrusive deployment across any 
HTTP -based micro service. The framework is deployed 
as a Docker Compose stack consisting of multiple 
containerized components, including the API service, 
Prometheus for metric collection, an AI -based 
remediation engine, a chaos controller for infrastructure -
level fault injection, Toxiproxy for network fault 
simulation, a watchdog for self -monitoring, and Alert 
manager for rule -based alert forwarding.  
The system collects five key telemetry features at five -
second intervals: request rate, error rate, 95th percentile 
latency, memory usage, and active connections. While 
the first three represent traditional reliability indicators, 
memory and connection metrics enable early detection of 
issues such as memory leaks and deadlocks that may not 
immediately affect throughput. Metrics are normalized  
using a scalar  fitted during training, and a sliding 
window of 30 time steps  (150 seconds) is constructed to 
form supervised learning sequences for forecasting -based 
anomaly detection.  
Detection operates through a dual -layer mechanism. A 
rule-based layer rapidly identifies well -known fault 
signatures, such as HTTP 500 or 503 spikes, providing 
low-latency responses for discrete failures. In parallel, an 
LSTM model enhanced with Multi -Head Self -Attention 
learns normal behavioral  patterns and flags deviations 
using mean squared error between predicted and 
observed values. A coordination mechanism prevents 
duplicate remediation by prioritizing  rule-based triggers 
when active.  
When an alert is generated, a context -aware decision 
engine evaluates real -time CPU utilization  before 
selecting a corrective action. This prevents inappropriate 
restarts under high -load conditions, distinguishing 
between resource saturation and deadlock scenarios. 
Remediation actions are followed by active health 
verification to confirm successful recovery. The system 
supports multi -container routing, enabling protection of 
multiple services without code changes.  To ensure 
realistic evaluation, faults are injected exclusively at the 
infrastructure level using Docker resource constraints, 
process signals, network disconnections, container 
pausing, and Toxiproxy -induced latency. Scenarios are 
declaratively defined and executed through a chaos 
controller API, avoiding application -level fault 
simulation bias.  
To maintain reliability in dynamic environments, the 
system incorporates online fine -tuning on verified clean 
runtime data and dynamically recalibrates anomaly 
thresholds using live metric distributions. Additional 
safeguards include a watchdog process for engine 
recovery, log rotation for disk stability, cool down  
mechanisms to prevent restart oscillation, and 
corroboration filters to reduce false positives during low -
traffic periods.

IV. METHODOLOGY  
A.  Research Design Framework  
This work adopts a design science research methodology 
in which the central artifact —the Self -Healing AI 
Agent —is iteratively designed, implemented, and 
evaluated as an autonomous control system for cloud -
native micro services. The objective is not merely 
anomaly detection but the integration of monitoring, 
contextual reasoning, and automated remediation within 
unified closed -loop architecture. The methodological 
flow progresses through four phases: (i) analysis of 
micro service failure patterns and limitations of 
threshold -based monitoring, (ii) architectural design of a 
dual-layer detection and decision pipeline, (iii) 
implementation of containerized monitoring, inference, 
and remediation modules, and (iv) empirical validation 
through controlled infrastructure -level chaos 
experiments. The system is therefore evaluated 
operationally rather than solely through offline model accuracy metrics, aligning evaluation with real -world 
reliability requirements.  
B.  System Instrumentation and Data Acquisition  
The monitoring foundation of the system is built upon 
Golden Signal observability principles. The target 
FastAPI service is instrumented using middleware that 
records request count, error count, request latency 
distribution, and active request concurrency. These 
metrics are exported through a Prometheus -compatible 
/metrics endpoint and scraped at five -second intervals 
using a pull -based model. This separation ensures that 
the application remains unaware of the monitoring 
process, preserving architectural purity and deployment 
portability.  
The remediation engine retrieves telemetry data directly 
from the Prometheus HTTP API. For each inference 
cycle, a rolling 20 -minute time -series window is fetched 
at five -second resolution. Missing values during cold 
starts are zero -filled to ensure numerical stability. This 
acquisition strategy ensures temporal continuity while 
avoiding dependency on application -level 
instrumentation beyond standard metrics exposure.

D.  Dual -Layer Detection Architecture  
Detection is implemented through a hybrid architecture 
combining deterministic statistical rules with deep 
temporal modeling. The first layer consists of a rule -
based spike detector that executes PromQL queries over 
a 60-second look back window to compute peak HTTP 
error rates. If predefined thresholds are exceeded —for 
example sustained 500 or 503 error rates —the system 
immediately generates a fault alert. This layer provides 
rapid, interpretable detection for well -understood failure 
signatures and operates with a precedence window that 
temporarily suppresses AI -based detection to prevent 
duplicate remediation.

Memory (LSTM) network with a hidden size of 64 units 
for temporal forecasting. Rather than performing fault 
classification, the model predicts the next telemetry

F.  Context -Aware Remediation Strategy  
Upon confirmation of a fault alert, the Decision Engine 
performs contextual classification before executing 
infrastructure actions. A critical ambiguity exists for 
certain HTTP status codes, particularly 503 errors, which 
may arise from either resource saturation or application 
deadlock. These conditions require opposite remediation 
strategies. To resolve this ambiguity, the system queries 
real-time CPU utilization metrics from the Docker 
daemon and computes container CPU percentage using 
delta -based usage calculations. If CPU usage exceeds a 
defined threshold (default 70%), the condition is 
classified as overload and restart is suppressed to prevent 
capacity loss. If CPU remains below the threshold, the 
condition is classified as deadlock and container restart is 
initiated.  
Remediation is executed via Docker SDK calls to restart 
the target container. A cool down guard enforces a 
minimum interval between restarts to prevent oscillatory 
behavior. Following restart, the system enters a health 
verification loop, polling the service’s /health endpoint to 
confirm successful recovery before logging the 
remediation as verified.  
G.  Chaos Engineering Evaluation Method   
To validate robustness under realistic conditions, faults 
are injected exclusively at the infrastructure layer using 
Docker control primitives and network proxies. Fault 
types include process termination, CPU throttling, 
memory constraints, network disconnections, and latency 
injection via Toxiproxy. This approach ensures that 
anomalies arise from genuine infrastructure perturbations 
rather than artificial application logic branches. For each 
scenario, detection time, recovery time, false positive 
rate, and overload suppression accuracy are measured. 
Each experiment is repeated multiple times to compute 
mean and variance, ensuring reproducibility and 
statistical validity.  
H.  Reliability and Adaptive Safeguards  
To enhance operational stability, the system incorporates 
several protective mechanisms. A warm -up silence 
period suppresses detection during initial metric 
accumulation. Rule precedence prevents dual -layer 
conflicts. A rolling dynamic threshold adapts to evolving 
traffic patterns. Cool down enforcement prevents restart 
storms. Log rotation safeguards disk resources. Persistent 
dashboard statistics ensure continuity across restarts. 
Additionally, a watchdog process monitors the 
remediation engine itself and triggers restart if the agent 
fails, ensuring meta -level resilience.  
V. EXPERIMENTAL SETUP AND RESULT  
A. Experimental  Environment  
All experiments were conducted on a Windows 11 
workstation equipped with an Intel Core i7 processor (8 
cores) and 16 GB RAM. The complete system was 
deployed using Docker Compose, with the API service, 
Prometheus, Alertmanager, remediation engine, and 
chaos controller running as isolated containers within a 
shared Docker network. Metrics were scraped at five -
second intervals throughout all experiments. Model 
training and inference were executed on CPU using 
PyTorch 2.x under Python 3.11. Fault scenarios were 
injected at the infrastructure level using container control 
operations and network perturbations to ensure realistic 
runtime behavior.  
B. Model Training Outcomes  
The forecasting model was trained on 17,375 time -series 
sequences with a window length of 30 timesteps (150 
seconds). Validation mean squared error decreased from 
0.104 at the first epoch to a best value of 0.006535, 
representing a substantial reduction in forecasting error. 
Training converged within approximately 40 epochs, and 
early stopping prevented overfitting. The anomaly 
detection threshold was set using the 99th percentile of 
validation errors, ensuring that normal operational 
behavior remained below the decision boundary while 
preserving sensitivity to abnormal deviations.  
C. Detection Performance  
Detection latency was evaluated across seven fault 
scenarios, each repeated five times. Discrete faults —
including application crashes (500), deadlocks (503), 
timeouts (504), overload conditions, and bot traffic 
spikes (429) —were detected within 15 –25 seconds on 
average. This latency reflects the metric scrape 
resolution and alert confirmation window.  
Gradual degradation and memory leak scenarios were 
detected by the AI layer within 95 –112 seconds. 
Although slower than rule -based spike detection, these 
faults were identified significantly earlier than their 
natural failure point under uncontrolled conditions. 
Importantly, these gradual scenarios did not exceed static 
threshold limits during early stages, meaning they would 
not have been detected without the AI -based temporal 
model.  
D Recovery Performance  
Mean Time to Recovery (MTTR) was measured from 
fault injection to confirmed service restoration. For 
abrupt faults, recovery occurred within 31 –43 seconds on 
average. Behavioral anomaly scenarios exhibited longer 
MTTR values (118 –137 seconds), driven primarily by 
increased detection latency rather than restart execution 
time. Restart operations completed successfully across 
all runs. In two instances involving resource -intensive 
faults, verification exceeded the predefined polling 
window, though subsequent checks confirmed recovery 
shortly thereafter. Overall recovery verification within 
the defined window reached 90%.  
E. Context Classification and Stability  
Overload classification accuracy was evaluated across 
ten induced high -CPU scenarios. The system correctly 
suppressed restart actions in all overload cases, achieving 
100% classification accuracy. No incorrect restarts 
occurred under high -load conditions, and no false 
suppression occurred during normal operation.  
A two -hour continuous run under normal traffic 
conditions produced zero false remediations in the full 
system configuration. When AI -based detection operated 
without corroboration safeguards, anomaly alerts 
increased; however, with full decision logic enabled, no 
spurious recovery actions were triggered. This 
demonstrates that contextual filtering is essential for 
maintaining operational stability.  
F. Overall Performance Summary  
Across all evaluated scenarios, the system achieved rapid 
detection for discrete faults, early identification of 
gradual anomalies, reliable automated recovery, 100% 
overload suppression accuracy, and zero false 
remediations during sustained normal operation. Two of 
the evaluated fault categories were undetectable using 
rule-based monitoring alone, highlighting the necessity 
of integrating temporal anomaly detection within the 
remediation framework.  
VI. CONCLUSION AND FUTURE WORK  
This paper presented a self -healing AI system for 
automated monitoring and remediation of microservice -
based APIs. The framework combines fast rule -based 
spike detection with a temporal forecasting model to 
identify both abrupt service failures and gradual 
behavioral drift that traditional threshold monitoring 
often misses. A context -aware decision engine evaluates 
real-time resource utilization before executing recovery 
actions, allowing the system to distinguish between overload conditions and genuine application faults. 
Experimental evaluation demonstrated rapid detection of 
discrete failures, early identification of progressive 
anomalies, reliable automated recovery, and zero false 
remediations during sustained normal operation. The use 
of infrastructure -level fault injection further strengthened 
the validity of the results by ensuring realistic evaluation 
conditions.  
Overall, the findings indicate that integrating multi -layer 
anomaly detection with contextual decision logic 
provides a practical foundation for autonomous service 
recovery in containerized environments. By reducing 
recovery time while preserving operational stability, the 
system moves toward more adaptive and resilient cloud -
native operations. Future work will extend the 
framework to distributed multi -service environments, 
explore reinforcement learning for adaptive remediation 
policies, integrate with Kubernetes for production -scale 
deployment, and evaluate performance on real -world 
production trace datasets to further validate scalability 
and generalisability.
